Old Meets New

Modernising a veteran bank’s app for a new audience, without losing the loyal customers who built it.

About

A veteran banking institution asked us to redesign its app, which felt outdated next to current market standards. Two goals drove the project: bring the app up to modern standards and attract a new audience, while keeping the existing customer base.

The challenge

The app had to serve two parallel, somewhat conflicting needs: long-standing customers who wanted stability, personal service and simplicity, and a younger audience expecting an advanced, proactive and efficient digital experience.

The core challenge

Build one app that serves two different worldviews without losing either, while meeting the bank’s business goals: a shift in brand perception, customer satisfaction, more sales and operational agility.

The old app

Discovery workshop

Rather than jumping to solutions, we brought together 6 managers who work directly on the customer-facing app for a 4-hour design-thinking workshop.

  1. Mapping the current state Strategic goals and inspiration from leading financial products in Israel and abroad.
  2. Refining business goals Participants ranked every possible goal. The bank wanted innovation, but ranked brand perception and customer satisfaction well above it.
  3. Building personas With empathy maps we built two: Reuven, 50, an existing customer looking for warmth and stability, and Yaron, 35, the new audience looking for efficiency and minimal friction. We defined Yaron as a “soft techie”, not a stereotype.
  4. Mapping the customer journey A full month for both personas, then a vote on the shared key moments: checking the balance, taking loans, cash-flow worries, opening deposits, managing several accounts and handling irregular expenses.
  5. Turning it into direction A simple, personal interface for the existing audience alongside proactive tools for the new one, not as two apps but as layers in the same system.

User interviews

Before designing a single screen, we ran 10 in-depth interviews of up to 60 minutes each, 5 with existing customers and 5 with competitors’ customers, to test the workshop’s assumptions against real users.

Key insight

The gap between the personas was much smaller than the workshop assumed. Both wanted an end-to-end solution in the app. The real difference was how fast and how independently they wanted to get there.

Four pain points

  • Fragmented information Users wanted checking and credit cards in one view; card data was missing from the app.
  • No proactivity Users expected the bank to flag a problem before it happened, not report it afterwards.
  • Confusing savings products Technical terms pushed most interviewees to call a banker instead of using the app.
  • “Simple” actions needing a branch Raising a credit limit, international transfers, resetting a password.

“A digital bank needs to be accessible. Today, I need to get to my bank just to take care of the basics.”

Assaf S., interviewee

UX concept: the financial feed

Opening the app feels like scrolling a news feed, but bounded, not infinite. Account status at the top, insights and offers in the middle, banking products at the bottom. A structure, not a scroll.

  • A feed, not a maze Status, insights and products in one structure instead of scattered screens.
  • Proactive by default Insights and warnings appear before a problem happens.
  • A banker, one tap away Chat sits in the standard top-bar spot.
  • Built for how people manage money Credit cards now live in the app, and the checking account exports straight to Excel.

Wireframes

Creative concept: familiar, refreshed

User reviews kept repeating the same complaint: outdated, cluttered, not user-friendly, especially next to competitors. The brief was to modernise the feel without losing what existing customers already trust.

The existing brand language

  • Colour, dialled in The same blues and greens, refined from print-era values into a system built for screens, with clearer rules, more white space and more minimalism.
  • Typography, kept familiar Almoni stays: neutral, legible, accessible and already recognised by users.
  • Shape, kept soft Rounded corners throughout, signalling warmth and trust instead of sharp corporate edges.
  • Illustration, kept optimistic A flat style with subtle depth, warm and human, never competing with the content.

The result

One app with layers for both worlds: a calm, personal feed for long-standing customers, and proactive tools for those who want to move fast.
